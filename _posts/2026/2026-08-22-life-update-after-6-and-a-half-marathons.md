I turn 50 in September, so, naturally, I did what any reasonable person does at a milestone birthday: I dumped a decade of Strava data on an AI and asked it to tell me about myself. No goals, no specific question, just "here's `_activities`, what do you see." What came back was more interesting than I expected, so here it is.

### The numbers, zoomed out

10 years, 1,008 runs, 6,329 miles, 965 hours, 204,183 ft of climbing. Somewhere in there, 6 finished marathons and one that didn't finish — which is where the "6 and a half" in the title comes from.

Pace has drifted from 8:17/mi in 2018 to 9:47/mi this year, while weekly volume roughly doubled. Meanwhile average heart rate across runs actually **dropped**, from the low-160s (2018-2021) to the high-130s/low-140s now. Slower, at a lower heart rate, on more miles — the AI's read was that this is a mix of an aging max heart rate and a genuine shift toward easier, more aerobic running. Fair enough.

### The marathon that didn't happen

Last November's NYC Marathon has a Strava title of "Insult and Injury," which should have been a clue even before the AI pointed it out. Splits were solid through mile 14, then fell apart on the Queensboro Bridge descent into 1st Ave, and I stopped at mile 19. Doctors couldn't pin it down definitively, but the symptoms and the mile-by-mile blowup pattern point straight at IT band syndrome — a classic late-race, fatigue-and-downhill story. I'm running it again on November 1st, ten weeks from now, this time with hip/glute strength work and a cadence bump built into the plan instead of bolted on after the fact.

### The gym, the mileage, and the plot twist

Since last November I've also been at the gym about 5 days a week — a habit that, cross-referenced against the run log, turns out to have quietly cut my running volume roughly in half (21 mi/wk down to under 10) while my running consistency got measurably worse (gaps between runs went from ~2 days to ~3+, with several week-plus gaps that didn't used to happen). The AI's blunt assessment: the gym didn't add to the running, it competed with it.

### And, since we're doing full transparency here

I've also been dating someone much younger, since right after the marathon, and I now have a regular workout partner, which I assumed was fueling all of this newfound gym enthusiasm. The data agrees — just not in the direction I expected. Motivation is clearly up. It's just showing up as gym attendance, not running consistency. Turns out an accountability partner doesn't automatically go for a run with you.

### So, at 50

The honest summary an AI gave me, unprompted: you're fitter in some ways, less consistent in others, recovering from an injury you're not fully rehabbing, and about to run a marathon in ten weeks on a training block that fell apart last week. None of it was wrong. Time to go fix the parts that need fixing before November 1st.
